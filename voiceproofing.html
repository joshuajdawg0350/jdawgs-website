Why now

The numbers that built this category

Imposter scams are the most-reported fraud category in America: $2.95 billion in reported losses in 2024 inside $12.5 billion of total reported fraud (FTC Consumer Sentinel Data Book 2024) — rising to $3.5 billion in 2025 (FTC, June 2026). The FBI's IC3 created a dedicated AI-enabled fraud category in its 2025 report — 22,364 complaints, roughly $893 million — because impersonation by machine is now its own crime class. And the defense families were offered was "be careful." One projection, labeled as one: Deloitte (May 2024) projects US AI-enabled fraud losses reaching $40B by 2027.

Meanwhile the offense industrialized: a voice can be cloned from seconds of audio, and one employee in Hong Kong paid out roughly $25 million after a single video call with deepfaked executives. Carrier systems verify the phone number. Nobody verified the person — for households, nobody had even named the practice. Voiceproofing names it, and this page stocks it.

Set up a circle

Ten minutes, before the scary call

1 · Forge the words

Agree a code word and a duress word — in person, or on a call you placed. Never by text.

2 · Print the card

Fridge copy for the house, wallet copy for the road. Real callback numbers for everyone in the circle.

3 · Save the real numbers

The callback only works with numbers you already hold. Save them now, labeled.

4 · Run one drill

Beat the scripts once at the kitchen table, and they lose their power on the phone forever.

Stated plainly: Voiceproofing proves a caller holds your circle's secret. It cannot prove someone isn't being forced — that's what the duress word is for. The tools are free, carry no ads, and run entirely in your browser: no account, no tracking, nothing you type is sent anywhere.
