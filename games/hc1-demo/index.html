How to play

The Rules of the Street

You've got 30 days and $2,000 in your pocket — and you owe the Shark $2,000 at 10% a day. Pay it down, or it eats the run. Buy product cheap in one neighborhood, move it across the city, sell it high somewhere else. Every deal plays out right on the corner — watch for the scene panels. When day 30 ends, your score is your net worth: cash + bank + stash value − any debt you took on.

The Market

  • Prices are rolled fresh every day in every neighborhood, and each neighborhood only stocks some goods each day. "Not sold here" means no buying — but you can always sell what you carry, anywhere.
  • Every row has its own trade buttons — BUY 1, BUY 10, SELL 1 and SELL ALL. BUY 10 grabs up to ten at once (or as many as your cash and coat space allow), and SELL ALL clears everything you're holding of that good in a single tap.
  • Watch the ▲▼ arrows: they compare today's price to the last price you saw for that good.
  • Your coat holds 100 slots. Most goods take 1 slot per unit. A pint of Lean takes 4 slots.
  • Floods cut a price in half. Shortages spike it times four. The headlines tell you which is which.

Time & Travel

  • Moving to another neighborhood takes the rest of the day. Pick a spot on the tagged-up city map. When you arrive: interest hits, prices re-roll, and the day counter moves.
  • Wait a Day stays put, moves the day, and cools your heat by 1 star.
  • You start owing the Shark $2,000 — the same size as your starting cash. Debt grows 10% per day, compounding, until it's paid — pay it from the Money panel with the PAY buttons. Pay it off in full and the interest stops for good.
  • The Bank: stash cash in the bank with the DEPOSIT buttons and pull it back out with WITHDRAW. Banked cash earns 5%/day and can't be mugged — muggings and bust fines only ever touch the cash in your pocket, never your bank balance.

Flat Broke — The Shark

  • Run completely dry — no cash, nothing in the bank, nothing left to sell — and the run still isn't over. The Shark rolls up in the black Charger and makes you one offer: a $1,000 loan at 10%/day, stacked on whatever you already owe him. Take it and play on, or walk away and the run ends right there.
  • He offers exactly once. Take the money, go broke a second time, and it's game over — he won't ask twice. There's no borrowing while you've still got money: the Shark is your last option, not your bank.

Landmarks

  • Your robot levels up as your net worth climbs: First Rack at $1,000, Five Figures at $10,000, Clean the moment your debt hits $0, then Six Figures at $100,000, a Quarter Million at $250,000, The Block Is Yours at $500,000 — and at $1,000,000 you are the King of Hustle City. Each landmark fires once per run in a short video flash, and the robot is more pimped out every time.

Heat

  • The stars up top are your wanted level, 0 to 5. Big sales days, busts, and running from trouble raise it. Quiet days lower it.
  • Every good carries its own heat. Heavy, hot product in your coat raises your bust odds every single day you hold it — fentanyl most of all. Get busted holding fentanyl and you lose everything: stash and cash.
  • The hotter you run, the harder the named tiers come down: Patrol Officer Reyes on the quiet beats, Detective Voss on the busy ones, and Task Force Commander Kane when you're really loud.

The J-Dawg

  • The Boss Man's ride is a blacked-out boss sedan called the J-Dawg. You'll see it parked outside the Loan Shark HQ in Downtown.
  • Owe the Shark money and sit on it — deep in debt, late in the month, running hot — and the J-Dawg rolls up on you. You can pay up, run — a foot chase through the alleys, and getting caught costs like a bust — or face him and take what comes. Pay your debt off in full and he has no business with you: he never comes at all.
  • Pay the loan off in full and the visits stop. Still owe him on day 29 or 30 and he's still out there looking for you.
