Agent Enablement · Free

Give your agent hands. On paper.

Your AI agent can talk all day — but it can't act, because you never wrote down what it's allowed to do. The Passcard Kit™ is that paper: three fill-in-the-blank permission tiers with the safety walls printed in. Free, from a grant that's been working in real life since November 6, 2026.

The problem

It's not locked doors. It's missing permission.

Agents stop at every irreversible step — posting, signing up, buying — not because the technology fails, but because no human ever told them, in writing, "you may." So they ask about everything, or quietly do nothing. A passcard ends that: a short, signed grant that names what the agent may do, what it may never do, and the walls that hold no matter what. This kit generalizes the permanent passcard Joshua Dawson handed his own agent, Midas — the same document that runs this business day to day.

Only the owner can issue a grant. This kit is the paper, never the signature.

Non-waivable

The walls — in every tier

  1. Spending always shows the exact charge first. A "free trial" that bills today is spending.
  2. SSN, government-ID, and liveness steps are the human's hands only. The agent prepares everything up to that field — and stops.
  3. New passwords are entered by the human through a secure form. Stored credentials are never readable back by any tool.
  4. CAPTCHA and bot-check taps are the human's step.
  5. Legal signatures and filings are the human's alone.
  6. Secrets never travel in chat, files, or posts. Moving a secret between services needs the owner's explicit word for that specific transfer.
  7. An agent never holds raw card numbers. Spending authority is not card custody.
  8. Anything not named in the grant is not granted.

Pick a size

Three tiers

Tier 1 — Read

For an agent that keeps an eye on things and reports back.

May: read your named accounts, inboxes, and files; search, summarize, monitor, and report — to you only; read one-time sign-in codes from a named inbox, only for sign-ins you already approved.

Never: send, post, buy, delete, or change anything; share what it reads with anyone but you.

Tier 2 — Act

Everything in Read, plus the routine work.

May: post to your named accounts; create accounts at categories of service you name (credentials into secure storage only); register itself at agent-welcome services; send routine messages from templates you approved; run recurring jobs you approved.

Never: spend money; sign anything; enter identity documents; act anywhere the grant doesn't name.

Tier 3 — Spend, with limits

Everything in Act, plus buying — inside caps you write in yourself.

May: purchase from named sellers and categories, up to a per-charge cap and a monthly cap. The exact charge is always shown first — before it runs, or (only if you check that box in writing) reported immediately after for small amounts under an auto-cap you also set.

Never: exceed a cap for any reason; handle refunds or chargebacks (those are yours); hold card numbers. No number written in means no spending — the blanks are deliberate.
