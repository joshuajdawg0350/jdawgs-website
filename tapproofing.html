Why this category exists now

For decades, a message that looked right probably was right. That ended when scam kits learned to clone any delivery notice, toll bill, bank alert, or job offer — perfectly, for pennies. The platforms check upstream, and browsers warn after the tap. Nothing served the 30 seconds before the tap, on your own phone, with the actual message in your hand. So we built it — the whole shelf, free.

$470 million

Reported losses to scams that started with a text message in 2024 — more than five times the 2020 figure, even as reports fell (FTC Data Spotlight, April 2025). Fake package delivery was the #1 text scam.

#1 first contact

In 2025 fraud reports, a text message was the most common way scammers first reached people (FTC) — the text is now fraud's front door.

~$389 million

Reported 2025 losses to crypto-kiosk scams (FBI IC3) — a script that ends with the victim scanning the scammer's QR code. The FBI has also warned (July 2025) about QR codes mailed inside unsolicited packages.

194,000+

Malicious domains tied to one smishing kit impersonating toll agencies, postal services, and banks (Palo Alto Unit 42, 2025). The supply is industrial. The household defense was a warning article — until now.

The 30-second rule

Stop. Don't tap. Check. Any message that wants a tap, a scan, a reply, or a payment gets 30 seconds of proof first: run the link, read the message, decode the code. Then — if it survives — reach the service through its own app or by typing the address yourself, never through the message.
